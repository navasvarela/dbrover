DbRover and other database tools

There are many good database tools, and most people already use one. This page says what DbRover offers, what it doesn't do yet, and how its features line up with DBeaver Community, DataGrip, TablePlus and DBX, so you can tell whether it suits you.

What DbRover brings

Native, free and open

Written in Rust and drawn by egui, with no browser engine or JVM inside. It opens in about a third of a second on a recent Mac, runs on macOS, Windows and Linux, and is MIT or Apache-2.0 licensed, with no account to create.

AI assistants, kept in bounds

A built-in MCP server, off until you allow a project. On PostgreSQL, MySQL, MariaDB and SQLite the database itself keeps an assistant's reads read-only, and writing, changing the schema or admin each need your permission.

Yours to bend

Safety levels per data source, a history of every statement down to the assistant that sent it, and settings in plain files, including actions that run your own programs on the rows you select.

What DbRover doesn't do yet

  • ER diagrams, schema compare, a visual explain plan and charts. All four are on the roadmap.
  • Signing in with a cloud account; that is planned, AWS first.
  • Oracle, which is planned.
  • Export beyond CSV and JSON; Excel, Parquet, Markdown, SQL and formats of your own are planned.
  • An AI chat inside the app. DbRover works with the assistant you already use.
  • Syncing settings between machines; data sources move with an exported file.

When another tool may suit you better

  • DataGrip has the deepest SQL editor of the lot, with inspections and refactoring across your scripts.
  • DBeaver connects to more than a hundred databases and has a large community behind it.
  • TablePlus is a polished native app with charts and ER diagrams you can edit.
  • DBX is free and quick, with editable ER diagrams, schema compare and more than a hundred databases.
  • If you need Oracle or MongoDB today, any of these four has them.

Every fact on this page, with its source, is in the research behind it. These tools change often; if something here is out of date, open an issue and it will be corrected.
